Two dangers, judged separately

Two different dangers have two different answers. Paw Weather judges them separately and shows you the worse of the two, so the reason you get is the reason that matters.

Paws — pavement temperature

Estimated from your local forecast (the National Weather Service in the United States, MET Norway everywhere else), sun angle, cloud cover and surface type (asphalt or concrete), plus the pavement’s own thermal lag.

Pavement thresholds for an average adult dog.
PavementVerdictWhat it means
under 110°FGO—
110°F+CAUTIONGrass, shade, shorter route
125°F+DANGERSkin damage in about a minute

Puppies get 5°F stricter thresholds — thinner pads. Concrete sidewalks run 14–25°F cooler than asphalt in the same sun — 20°F or more once the air is above 80°F — and the app shows both numbers so you can just cross the street.

Heat — heatstroke risk

The heat index: temperature plus humidity, because a dog cools itself by panting and humid air makes that fail.

Heat index thresholds for an average adult dog.
Heat indexVerdictWhat it means
under 80°FGO—
80°F+CAUTIONKeep it short and easy
90°F+DANGERHigh heatstroke risk

Stricter for the dogs that overheat first: flat-faced breeds −6°F, puppies and seniors −4°F, a dark coat in direct sun −3°F (10°F stricter at most). Air at 100°F or above is DANGER regardless.

Cold — ice, road salt and wind chill

Shaded pavement freezes on clear, calm nights before the air does, so the app keeps a separate estimate for it. Feels-like below 32°F is CAUTION and below 15°F is DANGER for an average adult dog — stricter for small, short-coated, young and senior dogs, with more room for double-coated breeds.

Why “walk after sunset” isn’t automatically safe

Asphalt holds its heat. When the model was checked against 16 Arizona DOT roadway sensors about 3.7 hours after sunset, the pavement was still 6.8°F above air temperature on average, up to 10.1°F — and it was warmer than the air at all 16 stations. Paw Weather carries that lag forward hour by hour, which is exactly why its evening windows are narrower than a plain temperature forecast would suggest.

Pavement numbers are shown rounded to 5°F. That is deliberate: it is an estimate, and rounding keeps it from looking more precise than it is.

The curve on the front page, as numbers

The same values the chart is drawn from, produced by running the app’s own risk engine. Clear sky, 40% relative humidity, and the hottest hour of the day — pavement stores heat, so the app solves a whole day rather than a single moment. The representative day is 35°N, 98°W on 15 July, with the air temperature holding steady; a real forecast moves it.

Questions

Where does the weather come from?

In the United States, from the National Weather Service (api.weather.gov), a public-domain service. Everywhere else, from MET Norway (api.met.no), licensed CC BY 4.0. So the forecast and the verdict work outside the United States too. From version 1.5.4, rain for the next 60 minutes during a walk comes from Apple Weather.

How do I move my records to a new iPhone?

From version 1.5.4, if you are signed in to iCloud with iCloud Drive turned on, Paw Weather keeps a copy of your records and photos in your own iCloud Drive. Sign in to the same Apple Account on the new iPhone, install Paw Weather, and your records follow. You can also save a backup file yourself from Settings → Back up records.

How does it look on iPhone Duo?

From version 1.5.4, folded, you get the usual screen. Unfolded, the right side stays as the folded screen and the left side shows the details — the next 18 hours, or rain for the next 60 minutes on a walk. In Learn, the articles are listed on the left and the page opens on the right. During a walk, the sidebar on the hinge widens the map to one big pane.

I bought Pro and it isn’t showing up

Open Settings → Restore purchases. Pro is tied to the Apple ID that bought it, so restoring works on any device signed in to that Apple ID. If it still doesn’t unlock, email me and I'll help.

It keeps suggesting walk times I'd never take

Settings → Walk hours caps the earliest (5–8 AM) and latest (7–11 PM) times Paw Weather will ever suggest. Walk windows and alerts stay inside those hours — set them to your routine and to the streets you trust after dark.

What about cold, ice and road salt?

In cold weather Paw Weather warns about frostbite risk on bare paws, wind chill, ice and road salt conditions, and reminds you to wipe your dog’s paws after the walk. How strict the cold verdict gets depends on your dog’s coat and breed.

Does it collect my data?

No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. Dog profiles and settings stay on the phone. If you allow location, the coordinates go only to the public weather service for your area — the National Weather Service in the United States, MET Norway elsewhere — to fetch the forecast. Limits

Pavement temperatures are estimates. They are modelled from the forecast, the sun’s angle and cloud cover — not measured on your street. Surface age, color, wind and shade move the real number by 10°F or more. The 7-second hand test always has the final say.

Paw Weather is an educational planning tool, not veterinary advice. If your dog shows signs of a paw burn or heatstroke, contact a veterinarian right away.
