How it works

Two dangers, judged separately.

Hot pavement (paw burns) and heat (heatstroke) are different dangers with different answers. Paw Weather judges each one and shows you the worse of the two, so the reason you see is the reason that matters.

Summer thresholds for an average adult dog
What it checksCautionDanger
Paws — estimated pavement110°F+125°F+
Body — heat index80°F+90°F+
Air temperature itself—100°F+

Puppies get pavement limits 5°F stricter. Heat limits are stricter for flat-faced breeds (−6°F), puppies and seniors (−4°F) and a dark coat in direct sun (−3°F), 10°F stricter at most.

Winter: ice, road salt and cold

On clear, calm nights shaded pavement freezes before the air does. Feels-like (wind chill) below 32°F is CAUTION and below 15°F is DANGER — stricter for small, short-coated, young and senior dogs, with more room for double-coated breeds.

Where the weather comes from

The National Weather Service in the United States, MET Norway everywhere else. Rain for the next 60 minutes during a walk comes from Apple Weather. Pavement temperature is an estimate from sun angle, cloud cover and the heat the pavement has stored.

FAQ

Questions dog owners ask

How hot is too hot for a dog's paws on pavement?

At about 125°F, pavement can injure a dog's paw pads in about 60 seconds of contact. For an average adult dog, Paw Weather says CAUTION from 110°F pavement and DANGER from 125°F. Puppies get thresholds 5°F stricter because their pads are thinner.

At what air temperature is asphalt too hot for dogs?

On a clear day, Paw Weather's model puts asphalt at 125°F when the air is about 76°F, at the hottest hour of the day. Published spot measurements put asphalt near 125°F at 77°F air. A concrete sidewalk runs 14–25°F cooler than asphalt in the same sun.

What is the 7-second rule for dog walks?

Press the back of your hand on the pavement for seven seconds. If you can't hold it there, it is too hot for your dog's paws. Paw pads burn at about the same temperature as human skin. The app's pavement numbers are estimates, so your hand has the final say.

When is it too hot to walk a dog because of heatstroke?

Paw Weather judges heatstroke risk on the heat index, which combines temperature and humidity. For an average adult dog it says CAUTION from a heat index of 80°F and DANGER from 90°F. It is stricter for flat-faced breeds, puppies, seniors and dark coats in direct sun. Air at 100°F or above is DANGER regardless of humidity.

Is it safe to walk my dog after sunset in summer?

Not automatically. Asphalt stores heat. Checked against 16 Arizona DOT roadway sensors about 3.7 hours after sunset, the pavement was still 6.8°F above the air on average, and up to 10.1°F. Paw Weather carries that stored heat forward hour by hour when it suggests walk times.

What should I watch for on winter dog walks?

On clear, calm nights shaded pavement can freeze while the air is still above freezing. Road salt dries and cracks paw pads and makes dogs sick if they lick it off, so wipe your dog's paws after every walk. Paw Weather warns about ice, road salt conditions and frostbite risk, and adjusts for your dog's size and coat.

Does Paw Weather work outside the United States?

Yes. In the United States the forecast comes from the National Weather Service; everywhere else from MET Norway. Minute-by-minute rain during a walk comes from Apple Weather. The app is in English and Japanese and shows °F or °C.

General safety information, not veterinary advice. If your dog shows signs of a paw burn, heatstroke or frostbite, contact a veterinarian right away.
